## 3. Parts 2 and 3: the declaration, the lowering, the editor

- **Declaration.** `TERRAIN_PASSES` = `walkable, blocked, needs, one-way`, plus `ONE_WAY_PASS` and `TERRAIN_DIRS` =
  `N, S, E, W` (the engine's input order).
  - A terrain entry with `pass: "one-way"` carries **`dirs`, the MOVE directions that may ENTER the cell**: `["E"]` is
    entered moving east, i.e. from the west. Leaving is free. I chose this form because it is the engine's own key:
    `entry_gates`' `dir` is the move's direction.
  - Refused by name: `dirs` missing, empty, not a direction, a duplicate, all four (that is `walkable`), and `dirs` on
    any other pass.
  - `terrainOf` returns `dirs` in canonical order.
- **Lowering.** `lowerTileKind` writes `{x, y, dir, gate: null}` for every other direction into `world.entryGates`,
  copy-on-write (`cloneWorld` shares the Map by reference, the same trap PASS-2 met with `obstacleLib`).
  `liftTileKind` removes them, and deletes the Map when it is empty, so placing and erasing is byte-equal.
- **The world's copy is the cell's gates.** `tileKindPassAt` reads `one-way` off a cell that has a `null` gate, and
  `tileKindDirsAt` reads the directions that may enter. The payload writes `{x, y, kind, pass: "one-way", dirs}`.
- **Ownership** (`ownedEntryGatesRefusal`, in `withTileKinds` and the round trip's save). A one-way kind's cell holds
  exactly a `null` gate for every direction not in `dirs`, and nothing else. Any other kind's cell holds no entry gate.
  Both are refused by name. A non-canonical `dirs`, or `dirs` and `one-way` not written together, is malformed.
- **The serializer and the round trip** (§ 5.1). `serializeMazeWorld` writes `entry_gates` only for a world whose
  placed kinds own gates. When a one-way kind is involved on either side, the re-stamp merges the document's own gates
  with the edit's owned ones; otherwise it keeps its old rule.
- **Look/logic.** A wall look is refused (it is not blocked), and so is a plain floor look (it is not walkable, and
  would claim the cell is free both ways). Marker and character looks are allowed.
- **Editor.**
  - `setTileKind` lowers the gates and says `(one-way: entered moving N or S only)`.
  - It refuses a cell carrying entry gates of the world's own (`foreign-entry-gates`).
  - Replacing or erasing a kind lifts its gates exactly.
  - Painting floor over a one-way kind is refused (`one-way-tile-kind`, "erase the kind first").
  - Copy/paste re-lowers the gates at the destination.
- **Drift.** A pass reads `one-way E` or `one-way N,S`, so a changed `dirs` is drift. A broken gate set is named
  (`tile-kind-gate-mismatch`).

## 4. The refusals

| Where | Reason | Sentence |

| structure parser | all four directions | `dirs [...] lets every move in: that is pass "walkable", not "one-way"` |
| structure parser | `dirs` on another pass | `dirs ["E"] is only for pass "one-way" (this entry's pass is "blocked")` |

### 5.1 What the round trip needed (found by reading the code)

The lab's library capture serializes rooms with `serializeMazeWorld`, which did not write `entry_gates`. Only the atlas
projection and the lab level record (`serializeMazeEntities`) write them. A one-way kind's gates would therefore have
been lost between the lab and the save, and a removed one would have been put back by the re-stamp's "copy back
whatever the original had" rule.
- `serializeMazeWorld` now writes `entry_gates` only when a placed kind owns gates. No committed world places a kind, so
  no committed payload moves; the op-log and producer checks confirm it.
- The re-stamp takes the document's gates minus those its one-way kinds owned, then the edit's (which are its own
  kinds' gates, since the capture drops the rest). It does this only when a one-way kind is involved on either side, so
  the Seedling levels' round trip keeps its exact old behaviour; the sidecar tally confirms it.

## 8. What the code overturned

1. **"Recompile every committed preset and diff them" was not the whole measurement.** The committed presets did not
   move, but the generic extractor's own derivation of four Seedling regions did (§ 2 d). Only a direct extract-and-
   compile probe over every committed region showed it. It is recorded, and continuing was ruled.
2. **The registry serializer never wrote `entry_gates`.** The lab and round-trip path would have lost a one-way kind's
   lowering, and the re-stamp would have undone its removal (§ 5.1). The design's "lowered at write" needed the
   serializer and the re-stamp taught, under a condition that leaves every committed payload and every Seedling round
   trip byte-for-byte as before.
3. **`floorReachableSet` is a fifth reader** that the design's table did not list. It promises `ghostStep`'s semantics,
   so it reads `null` gates too.
4. **Two readers stay blind on purpose**, and are documented in `gotchas.md`: `longestShortestPath` (a baked payload
   metric) and the visualizer's blocked-step text.
5. **`cloneWorld` shares `entryGates` by reference**, as it does `obstacleLib` (PASS-2), so the lowering is
   copy-on-write.
6. **A kind may not sit on a cell with the world's own entry gates.** Otherwise the cell's gates could not say which
   were the kind's. A non-one-way kind on a gated cell is refused at load as well.
